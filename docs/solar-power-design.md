# Solar and battery design — revision 0.2

Per node: 12.8 V 20 Ah LiFePO4 BAT512020705, SmartSolar MPPT 75/10, Smart BatteryProtect 65 A BPR065022000 and two **20 W SPM040201200 panels in series**. Two nodes therefore require four panels. The 20 W model is discontinued but Australian remaining stock was listed on 9 November 2026; stock confirmation is a procurement gate. A different panel requires new voltage/protection calculations, even if its wattage is similar.

The model allocates continuous 80 mA at 3.3 V for processor, radio and board; it claims no unverified CPU sleep saving from Thread MTD. Three monitors receive a conservative 1 mA allowance each. The 17.2 V, 24 mA loop runs 7 s every 300 s, with 80% boost and 85% buck efficiencies. The charger’s 20 mA standby is included, as are BatteryProtect’s 1.4 mA with BLE on, a 2 mA unverified BMS allowance, 200 µA reverse-controller and 150 µA raw-presence allowances, regulator quiescent allowance, reverse diode loss and pullups/LEDs. Battery self-discharge is an explicit 3%/month design allowance, not a verified specification of this pack.

Run `python3 tools/power_budget.py`; the component allocations and 5/10/20/40 W sensitivity results are saved in `tests/test-results/power-budget-v02.json`. About 16.47 Wh/day is allocated. Seven days plus 25% margin requires about 144.09 Wh. Applying both 80% usable discharge and 80% aging/cold capacity to 256 Wh leaves 163.84 Wh. This passes the model, subject to measured current and cold capacity. Cutoff voltage is not a reliable LiFePO4 state-of-charge measurement.

At 1.5 equivalent sun hours and 60% total harvest derating, 5 W and 10 W are deficient. A 20 W array has little recharge surplus. The selected 40 W array gives 34.83 Wh/day after a conservative 1.2 V series-diode allowance and roughly 7.85 modelled days to replenish the conservative seven-day reserve while serving its load. Extended cloud and site shading can defeat this average; it is not an autonomy guarantee.

NASA POWER monthly climatology for a regional Maiden Gully point (−36.76, 144.22) is preserved in `docs/data/nasa-power-climatology.json`. Its 2001–2025 June mean is approximately 2.135 kWh/m²/day; July 2.287. The lower 1.5 sun-hour design assumption is retained. This is regional radiation, not a tank-site shade survey or a measured tilted-panel yield. [NASA service documentation](https://power.larc.nasa.gov/docs/tutorials/service-data-request/api/).

Two 20 W panels have 37 V STC Vmp and 45.2 V Voc. With −0.35%/°C Voc coefficient, −10°C cell Voc is 50.737 V, below 75 V charger input and 51 V TVS standoff. A conservative estimated hot Vmp of 24.922 V after D9 at 85°C remains above 14.6 V battery plus 5 V charger startup headroom. The hot Vmp coefficient is inferred from Pmax/Isc coefficients and requires an actual hot I–V test. The current 30 W SPM040303603 is **not a drop-in**: two series panels exceed the chosen PV TVS standoff in the same cold calculation.

Configure charger battery chemistry, absorption/float limits, equalisation disabled and charge-current limit from the exact battery’s current instructions. The 20 Ah pack’s low-temperature charge inhibit and BMS production version require written supplier confirmation before procurement; do not substitute the specifications of Victron NG batteries. Do not charge below the pack’s permitted temperature. The INA228 die-temperature entity is not battery temperature.

Configure BatteryProtect in voltage-threshold mode A with user-defined preliminary 12.0 V disconnect / 13.0 V reconnect, validate under load, and adjust only within the battery manufacturer’s limits. Do not select mode C merely because the battery is lithium: mode C disables voltage thresholds and requires an external BMS control signal. Its IN is after the battery shunt, OUT only powers the board, and its GND lead returns to battery negative. Charger current must never flow backward through BatteryProtect. Keep charger BAT connected so solar can recover a BatteryProtect-disconnected load while the battery BMS remains active. A fully off BMS cannot automatically recover through the new reverse-blocking MOSFETs; follow the raw-battery recovery procedure in electrical-design.md. The battery BMS remains connected even when the board is off.
